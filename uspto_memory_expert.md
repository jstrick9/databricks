SECTION 1: WHAT IS A TRADEMARK?
================================================================================

## Definition
- Any word, phrase, symbol, design, or combination identifying goods or services
- Identifies source of goods/services; distinguishes from competitors
- Can refer to both trademarks (goods) and service marks (services)
- Not ownership of a word/phrase generally—only for specific goods/services
- Common misconception: choosing descriptive marks is effective (false—creative/unique marks are more effective and easier to protect)

## Types of Marks
- Standard character marks: text only (TM/SM can be used before filing; ® after registration)
- Design marks (logos, symbols) may include color claims
- Collective marks, certification marks, collective membership marks
- Service marks for services; trademarks for goods

## Ownership vs. Registration
- Become owner as soon as you start using the mark in commerce
- Common law rights: limited to geographic area of use
- Federal registration creates nationwide rights
- State registration: only protects within that state
- International: Madrid Protocol allows single application for 100+ countries (not automatic approval—each country reviews independently)

## Trademark Symbols
- TM: goods (can use without filing)
- SM: services (can use without filing)
- ®: registered with USPTO (only for goods/services listed in registration)
- Symbols can be superscript/subscript to right of mark

SECTION 2: WHY REGISTER YOUR TRADEMARK?
================================================================================

## Benefits of Federal Registration
1. Listed in USPTO database (public notice)
2. Legal presumption of ownership (registration certificate proves ownership in federal court)
3. Basis for foreign trademark protection
4. Right to bring lawsuit in federal court
5. Use of ® symbol
6. Record with U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) to stop infringing imports
7. Nationwide rights (not limited to geographic use area)
8. Can claim incontestability (Section 15) after 5 years continuous use—stronger protection

## Common Law Rights vs. Federal vs. International
- Common law: based solely on use in commerce; only enforceable where used; not in USPTO database
- Federal (USPTO): nationwide; listed in database; use ® symbol; rely on rights when expanding
- State: protects only within that state; not all states have registration databases
- International (Madrid Protocol): single application for multiple countries; no "worldwide" trademark—each country decides independently

## Duration
- Can last forever with continued use in commerce and timely maintenance filings
- Section 8 declaration (5-6 years), Section 8 & 9 renewal (9-10 years, then every 10 years)
- Section 71 for Madrid-based registrations
- Six-month grace periods available with additional fees
- Failure to file results in cancellation/expiration

SECTION 3: TRADEMARK BASICS - KEY CONCEPTS
================================================================================

## Strong Trademarks
- Creative and unique marks are more effective and easier to protect
- Descriptive marks are weaker and harder to protect
- Arbitrary/fanciful marks offer strongest protection

## Scope of Protection
- Rights apply only to specific goods/services identified
- Similar marks for unrelated goods/services may coexist
- Example: woodworking logo doesn't prevent similar logo for non-woodworking goods

## Filing Bases (Sections of Trademark Act)
- Section 1(a): Use in commerce (currently using)
- Section 1(b): Intent-to-use (ITU) — bona fide intention; must file Statement of Use or Amendment to Allege Use later ($150/class fee)
- Section 44(d): Foreign application filed within 6 months (priority claim)
- Section 44(e): Foreign registration (must show use in U.S. within 3 years)
- Section 66(a): Madrid Protocol international registration

## Goods and Services Classification
- International Class system (45 classes: 1-34 goods, 35-45 services)
- Must select identifications from the Trademark ID Manual or use free-form text (free-form incurs additional fees)
- Per-class filing fees apply ($350 base per class as of 2025)

## Drawings and Specimens
- Standard characters: typed in appropriate field
- Non-standard: digitized image of mark (must include color claim and description if applicable)
- Specimens: evidence of use in commerce (photos of product/packaging, labels, tags, webpages with shopping cart, catalogs, advertisements, software screenshots)
- Digitally created/altered or mockup specimens are unacceptable

SECTION 4: SEARCH AND CLEARANCE
================================================================================

## Why Search?
- Avoid likelihood of confusion refusal
- Prevent opposition/cancellation proceedings
- Avoid trademark infringement lawsuits
- Check common law rights (internet, business names, state databases)

## Comprehensive Clearance Search Includes
- USPTO trademark search system (federal registered/pending)
- Trademark Official Gazette (TMOG) — weekly publication of approved marks
- U.S. state trademark/business registries (50 states + DC + PR)
- Domain name registries (ICANN)
- Madrid Monitor (international registrations)
- WIPO Global Brand Database
- EUIPO TMview (EU and partner countries)
- Internet searches (multiple search engines) for common-law use

## Federal Trademark Searching Tools
- TESS (Trademark Electronic Search System) — being replaced/supplemented
- Trademark Search System (tmsearch.uspto.gov) — modern search interface
- Field tag searching, regex searching available
- Design Search Code Manual for design elements

## Likelihood of Confusion
- Examined by USPTO examining attorney
- Similar marks with related goods/services = likely refusal
- Factors: similarity of marks, relatedness of goods/services, channels of trade, consumer sophistication, actual confusion evidence

## Coordinated Classes and Design Search Codes
- Related goods/services grouped by coordinated classes
- Design search codes help find similar design elements

SECTION 5: APPLICATION PROCESS
================================================================================

## Filing Requirements (Base Application)
- Applicant name, domicile address, email
- Legal entity (individual, corporation, LLC, etc.)
- Citizenship (individual) or state/country of incorporation (entity)
- Filing basis (Sections 1(a), 1(b), 44, 66(a))
- Filing fee per class
- Verified statement (signed by authorized person)
- Correctly classified goods/services (from ID Manual preferred)
- Drawing/specimen requirements
- If non-English: English translation; if non-Latin: transliteration
- If includes individual's name/likeness: written consent or statement it's not a living individual
- Multi-class applications must comply with 37 CFR 2.86
- If domiciled outside U.S.: attorney information required

## Additional Fees (2025 Fee Rule — Effective Jan 18, 2025)
- Base fee: $350/class (Section 1 or 44 applications meeting requirements)
- Insufficient information: +$100/class
- Free-form text box instead of ID Manual: +$200/class
- Additional groups of 1,000 characters beyond first 1,000 in free-form: +$200/class
- Section 1(b) ITU additional fees: Statement of Use ($150/class); Extension of time ($125/class)
- Petition to revive abandoned application: $250
- Deletion fee if deleting goods/services before maintenance accepted: required

## Identity Verification (Mandatory)
- Required for all USPTO.gov account holders filing/editing trademark forms
- Must complete one-time verification through ID.me (online: self-service or video chat agent) or paper notarized form
- Online options: self-service (selfie + ID + SSN + credit profile header); video chat (no selfie, live agent, upload 2 primary or 1 primary + 2 secondary IDs)
- Paper: notarized form mailed to USPTO Alexandria, VA; 2-3 weeks processing
- Name on USPTO.gov account must match verification document exactly
- Attorney support staff must be sponsored by verified attorney (since Jan 20, 2024, staff must also verify themselves independently)
- Sponsorship preserved during verification unless name changes
- If attorney withdraws sponsorship, staff lose access
- User roles: trademark owner, U.S.-licensed attorney, Canadian attorney/agent, attorney support staff, interested party (beta for TTAB Center)
- Multi-factor authentication required for login security
- Warning: Don't verify an account someone created for you—scam risk
- Fax filing discontinued July 1, 2026; alternative: mail, hand delivery, or electronic (if deadline issue)

## Filing Process Steps
1. Search and clearance
2. Identify goods/services and select filing basis
3. Create USPTO.gov account and verify identity
4. Complete application (Trademark Center or TEAS)
5. Pay fees
6. Receive filing receipt and serial number
7. Examination by examining attorney
8. Respond to office actions (if any)
9. Publication in Official Gazette (30-day opposition period)
10. Receive Notice of Allowance (ITU applications) or proceed to registration
11. File Statement of Use (if ITU) or maintain registration

## Trademark Center
- As of Jan 18, 2025: file new applications, pay fees, docket tracking
- TEAS (Trademark Electronic Application System) being phased out/replaced for some functions
- Voluntary amendment form in TEAS retiring Sept 1, 2026
- TEASi for international applications transitioning to Madrid e-Filing Oct 1, 2026 (with transition period July 31 - Sept 30, 2026)
- TEAS forms include: base applications, response forms, ITU forms, post-approval amendments, correspondence/attorney forms, petitions, expungement/reexamination forms, miscellaneous, maintenance/renewal/correction, assignment, TTAB forms, Madrid Protocol forms

## System Availability and Filing Deadlines
- Eastern Time controls filing deadlines (11:59 p.m. ET = that day's date regardless of business hours)
- Check system status page before filing
- Most TEAS filings uploaded to TSDR within 4-5 business days
- Alternative filing methods available if electronic filing unavailable

SECTION 6: EXAMINATION PROCESS
================================================================================

## Office Action Timeline Changes (TMA)
- Shorter response period: 3 months (implemented Dec 3, 2022 for pre-registration; applies to Section 66(a) Madrid applications as well)
- Single 3-month extension available for fee ($125/class, same fee as ITU extension)
- Failure to respond within 3 months (plus any granted extension) = abandonment
- Previously 6 months; now 3 months to improve efficiency

## Examination Process
- Examining attorney reviews application for legal and procedural compliance
- Checks for conflicts with existing marks (likelihood of confusion)
- Checks for descriptiveness, deceptiveness, geographic descriptiveness, surnames, ornamental use, etc.
- Issues Office Action (non-final or final) listing problems
- Applicant responds within 3 months (plus optional 3-month extension for fee)
- May receive final refusal if issues not resolved
- Can appeal final refusal to Trademark Trial and Appeal Board (TTAB) or petition to Director

## Letter of Protest (Third-Party Submissions)
- Third parties can submit evidence before registration relevant to refusal grounds
- TMA provided statutory authority for this longstanding practice
- 2-month deadline for USPTO to act on submissions
- Director's decision is final and non-reviewable
- Fee applies

## Responding to Office Actions
- Must address each refusal/rejection
- Can submit arguments, amend application, provide additional evidence, delete goods/services
- If wrong form filed initially, may request substitute form
- Post-registration office actions have different timelines (6 months or end of statutory filing period, whichever is later)
- Third office action possible if issues remain after response
- Petition to Director available for review of certain determinations

## Common Problems in Applications
- Insufficient identification of goods/services
- Incorrect classification
- Descriptive or generic marks
- Likelihood of confusion
- Improper specimens
- Incorrect filing basis
- Incomplete information
- Wrong form type selected
- Missing consent for name/likeness
- Failure to verify identity
- Fraudulent or mistaken attorney designations (invalid if attorney didn't authorize)

================================================================================
SECTION 7: PUBLICATION AND OPPOSITION
================================================================================

## Approval for Publication
- Examining attorney approves application
- Published in Trademark Official Gazette (TMOG) — weekly publication
- 30-day opposition period begins upon publication
- Notice of Publication issued

## Opposition Proceedings (TTAB)
- Any party can file opposition within 30 days (or with extension)
- TTAB (Trademark Trial and Appeal Board) handles inter partes proceedings
- Grounds include likelihood of confusion, dilution, fraud, abandonment, descriptiveness, etc.
- More costly and time-consuming than ex parte proceedings
- TTAB proceedings search tool available (Decisions and Proceedings search tool)

## Cancellation Proceedings
- Can cancel registered marks for abandonment, nonuse, fraud, likelihood of confusion, etc.
- TTAB handles inter partes cancellation
- TMA added new expungement/reexamination proceedings (ex parte, faster, less expensive)

SECTION 8: NOTICE OF ALLOWANCE AND ITU APPLICATIONS
================================================================================

## Intent-to-Use (ITU) Process
- Section 1(b) applications receive Notice of Allowance (NOA) after approval
- Must file Statement of Use (SOA) or Amendment to Allege Use (AAU) within 6 months of NOA
- Can file up to 5 extension requests (6 months each, total 3 years from NOA) — $125/class per extension
- Must submit specimen showing actual use in commerce
- If not filed within deadlines, application abandoned
- Once SOA/AAU accepted, proceeds to registration

## Post-NOA Amendments
- Can request amendments after approval/publication/extension to oppose filed/NOA issued
- Forms available for different stages
- Must maintain accurate records—deletions before maintenance accepted require fee

SECTION 9: REGISTRATION AND POST-REGISTRATION
================================================================================

## Receiving Registration
- Certificate issued upon successful completion (use-based or ITU with SOA)
- Registration number assigned
- Listed in USPTO database (public notice)
- Use of ® symbol permitted
- Can record with CBP
- Registration can be used as basis for Madrid Protocol international applications

## Post-Registration Maintenance (Keeping Registration Alive)
### Required Filings (Non-Madrid/Section 66(a))
- Between 5th and 6th year: Section 8 Declaration of Use and/or Excusable Nonuse ($325/class; $575 combined with Section 15; grace period +$100)
- Between 9th and 10th year: Section 8 & 9 combined renewal ($650/class; grace period +$100)
- Every 10 years thereafter: Subsequent Section 8 & 9 renewals
- Section 15 Declaration of Incontestability (optional, $250/class): requires 5+ years continuous use; combined with Section 8 available; provides stronger protection (incontestability presumptions)
- Section 71 for Madrid Protocol registrations (5-6 years, 9-10 years, every 10 years; must renew international registration directly with WIPO IB every 10 years)

### Maintenance Filing Requirements
- Signed declaration that trademark is still in use with listed goods/services
- One specimen per class showing use
- Must evaluate actual use—delete goods/services no longer in use (no fee if done between maintenance filings; fee applies if deleted before maintenance accepted)
- If audited, must provide proof of use for all audited goods/services or delete them and pay deletion fee
- If entire registration canceled due to audit failure
- Grace periods available (6 months) with additional fees
- Must respond to post-registration office actions by deadline or registration canceled
- Petition to Director available for extraordinary circumstances

### Post-Registration Audit Program
- Random or targeted audits of maintenance filings
- Must provide proof of use for audited goods/services
- If unacceptable proof submitted: either delete (with fee) or provide proof for every item in audited class, or petition Director
- Third office action possible; must respond by deadline or entire registration canceled
- New proof of use cannot be submitted with response to third office action (only with petition to Director)
- Audit applies even if only some goods/services audited; entire registration at risk
- Examples of acceptable proof: hangtags, product labels, packaging, photographs, catalogs, webpages with shopping carts, software screenshots, brochures, advertising materials, tags
- Unacceptable: digitally created/altered/mockup proof; screenshots without URL/access date; generic descriptions without association to goods/services

### Common Mistakes in Maintenance
- Filing inaccurate declarations (listing goods not actually in use)
- Submitting unacceptable specimens (digitally altered/mockups)
- Missing deadlines (registration canceled)
- Failing to delete unused goods/services promptly (leads to fees and audit risk)
- Not updating correspondence address (miss notifications of expungement/reexamination petitions)
- Ignoring audit office actions
- Not paying deletion fees when required

### Section 7 Amendment/Correction
- Non-material corrections/amendments to registration certificate
- Must delete goods/services no longer in use promptly (before maintenance filing to avoid fees)
- Must keep registration accurate—legal requirement

### Excusable Nonuse
- Rare exception: special circumstances beyond control; no intent to abandon
- Must provide evidence (e.g., natural disaster preventing business operations, prolonged illness/hospitalization preventing filing)
- Not available for Section 1(a) use-based registrations in TMA proceedings (only for maintenance filings under specific conditions)
- Section 44(e)/66(a) registrations can claim excusable nonuse in expungement proceedings (must be in use within 3 years of registration)

SECTION 10: TRADEMARK MODERNIZATION ACT (TMA) OF 2020
================================================================================

## Overview
- Enacted 2020; regulations effective Dec 18, 2021
- Shorter office action response period (3 months) effective Dec 3, 2022
- Provides tools to clear unused trademarks from federal register
- Improves efficiency of examination process
- Small entity compliance guide included

## Expungement Proceedings (Ex Parte)
- Any third party can petition Director to cancel some/all goods/services
- Grounds: registrant never used the trademark in commerce with specified goods/services
- Time limit: between 3 and 10 years after registration date
- Must include: verified statement of reasonable investigation; index of evidence; evidence establishing prima facie case of nonuse; filing fee per class
- Director institutes if prima facie case established
- Registrant has 3 months to respond with proof of use (or excusable nonuse for Section 44(e)/66(a))
- Final determination: cancellation of challenged goods/services or denial of petition
- Non-institution determination is final and non-reviewable
- Same registration can also be subject to TTAB cancellation (inter partes—more expensive)

## Reexamination Proceedings (Ex Parte)
- Any third party can petition Director
- Grounds: trademark was not in use in commerce with specified goods/services as of relevant date
- Relevant date: filing date (for Section 1(a) use-based applications not amended to ITU); date of accepted AAU or end of SOU period (for ITU applications)
- Time limit: within first 5 years after registration
- Same petition requirements as expungement (reasonable investigation, evidence, index, fee)
- Director institutes if prima facie case established
- Registrant must respond with proof of use as of relevant date (cannot claim excusable nonuse for Section 1(a) registrations—statute requires use at filing for such registrations)
- Assignment issues: assignee responsible for establishing use by assignor as of relevant date

## Key Differences: Expungement vs. Reexamination
- Expungement: never used (any time 3-10 years); can claim excusable nonuse for foreign/Madrid registrations
- Reexamination: not used as of relevant date (first 5 years); no excusable nonuse for Section 1(a) registrations
- Both result in cancellation of only those goods/services where nonuse demonstrated (not entire class unless all challenged)
- Both faster and less expensive than TTAB inter partes proceedings
- Petitioner involvement ends after institution determination

## Letter of Protest (Pre-Registration Third-Party Evidence)
- Statutory authority under TMA (previously longstanding practice)
- Any person can submit evidence relevant to refusal grounds before registration
- Must be filed within 2 months of relevant publication/approval
- Fee applies
- Decision final and non-reviewable
- Director may institute on own initiative if evidence establishes prima facie case

## Attorney Designation Rules
- Any false, fraudulent, or mistaken attorney designation is not effective
- If applicant designates attorney without authorization, USPTO only communicates with applicant (attorney doesn't need to formally withdraw)

## Court Orders
- Procedures codified for action on court orders affecting registrations (15 U.S.C. § 1119)
- Requires certified copy; normally doesn't act until case finally determined

## TMA Impact on Maintenance Audits
- Owners must prove regular use; only provide one specimen per class unless audited
- Expungement/reexamination supplement existing audit/maintenance requirements
- Helps maintain register integrity by removing unused marks

SECTION 11: PROTECTION AGAINST SCAMS
================================================================================

## Common Scams
- Fraudulent solicitations from "IP experts" offering trademark services
- Spoofing phone numbers (scammers pretend to be from USPTO)
- False demands for payment (refer to real fees on website)
- Fraudulent filing firms (improperly entering applicant/attorney signatures)
- Foreign filing firms improperly soliciting U.S.-licensed attorneys
- Fraudulent trademark applications (more than 52,000 terminated as of recent updates)
- Online auction scams with counterfeit goods
- Challenge invalid specimens (fraudulent use evidence)
- Criminal conviction in trademark solicitation scam

## What USPTO Does
- Investigates suspected fraud, unauthorized practice of law, rule abuse
- Works with Department of Commerce OIG, DOJ, FTC, USPIS
- Issues administrative sanctions (orders for sanctions, suspension, preclusion of non-attorneys)
- Terminates fraudulent applications/registrations
- Requires identity verification to combat fraud
- Requires attorney sponsorship verification to combat abuse
- Doesn't have legal authority to sue/prosecute—works with federal agencies

## How to Protect Yourself
- Verify identity through official USPTO.gov/ID.me process only
- Never verify an account someone else created for you
- Be cautious of unsolicited offers for trademark services
- Verify attorney authorization before designating
- Check correspondence address is current
- Don't respond to unsolicited demands for payment
- Contact Trademark Assistance Center if suspicious
- Understand filing firms: some improperly solicit attorneys; some improperly file applications

## Administrative Sanctions Process
- Investigates violations
- Issues orders for sanctions
- Can suspend applications or preclude non-attorneys from filing
- Terminated more than 52,000 fraudulently filed applications

SECTION 12: SCAM PROTECTION - WHAT TO DO IF SCAMMED
================================================================================

- Report to USPTO (contact information available)
- Contact federal agencies (DOJ, FTC, USPIS)
- Don't pay scammers—USPTO never requests payment by phone, email, text, or social media
- Verify any communication is genuine through official channels
- Monitor application/registration status regularly through TSDR
- Update correspondence address promptly
- Work only with verified U.S.-licensed attorneys (check bar information)

SECTION 13: FEES AND PAYMENT
================================================================================

## Fee Structure (Per-Class Basis)
- Almost all fees calculated per class of goods/services
- Base application fee: $350/class (Section 1 or Section 44, if complete and uses ID Manual)
- Additional fees apply for incomplete/custom applications
- Section 1(b) ITU: Statement of Use ($150/class); Extension ($125/class)
- Maintenance: Section 8 ($325/class); Section 8 + 15 ($575/class); Section 8 + 9 ($650/class); grace period (+$100/class)
- Section 15 incontestability: $250/class
- Petition to revive: $250
- Deletion fee: applies when deleting goods/services before maintenance document accepted
- Processing fee for refused/charged back payment: $50
- Refunds generally not provided; filing doesn't guarantee registration

## Payment Methods
- Trademark Center: credit/debit card or USPTO deposit account
- TEAS/TEASi: Financial Manager system (credit/debit, deposit account, electronic funds transfer)
- Alternative filing/payment options available during system outages
- Only U.S. dollars accepted

## Fee Schedule Updates
- Fiscal year 2025 final fee rule effective Jan 18, 2025
- Changes include additional fees for incomplete/custom identifications

SECTION 17: BEST PRACTICES SUMMARY
================================================================================

1. Search comprehensively before filing (USPTO database, internet, state databases, domain names, international sources)
2. Choose strong, creative, unique marks (not merely descriptive)
3. Identify goods/services precisely using ID Manual
4. Verify identity before filing; don't create accounts for others
5. Select correct filing basis and provide accurate verified statement
6. Provide acceptable specimens showing actual use in commerce
7. Monitor application status regularly through TSDR
8. Respond to office actions promptly (3 months + optional 3-month extension)
9. File accurate maintenance documents at required intervals
10. Delete unused goods/services promptly (before maintenance filing when possible)
11. Maintain updated correspondence address
12. Be aware of audit risks; keep proof of use organized
13. Understand scams; verify attorney authorization; never pay unsolicited demands
14. Consider international protection through Madrid Protocol if expanding globally
15. Work with U.S.-licensed attorney for complex applications, responses, international filings, or disputes
16. Use official resources (TMEP, examination guides, webinars, boot camp) for education
17. Check system status before filing; file early within filing windows
18. Ensure accurate classification and drawing/specimen compliance to avoid additional fees
